Privacy

Updated on November 11, 2026.

Translated from the French version, which prevails if the two differ.

Mirinu is a personal project by Marco, in Switzerland. He is responsible for the data described here. For any question: contact@mirinu.ch.

On your device

The account is optional. Without an account, Mirinu asks for neither your name nor your email address. Your library, your ratings and your settings stay on your device: in the app on Android, in the browser's storage on app.mirinu.ch, without cookies. A file you import is read on the spot and sent nowhere.

On Android, if your phone's Google backup is on, it may copy your library, as it does for your other apps.

No ads, no trackers, no audience measurement.

What leaves your device

To show a title page or recognize the titles of an import, the app sends the title or number searched for to TMDB and MangaUpdates, directly or through the Mirinu relay. Images come from their servers (for some show posters, from TVmaze's), trailer thumbnails from Google's. On the website, text in a script that Mirinu's fonts don't include (Japanese, for example) downloads a fallback font from Google's servers. These services see your IP address, like any website, and follow their own rules.

For a comic, the app sends the title you search for or the ISBN read from the barcode to the Mirinu relay, which queries the Bibliothèque nationale de France and Wikidata. The camera is only used to read a barcode or another device's QR code: the image is read on the device and sent nowhere.

For a novel, the app sends the title or author you search for to the Mirinu relay, which queries Wikidata and the Bibliothèque nationale de France.

For a manga, the app sends the series number to the Mirinu relay, which queries MangaUpdates, the Bibliothèque nationale de France and Wikidata to find its official publishers. When you open a link in the “Where to read” section, your browser goes to the publisher's or platform's site: it sees your IP address and follows its own rules.

The “Help improve matching” setting, on at first, sends the relay the two numbers of an entry you pick during an import, with nothing that identifies you. You can turn it off in the settings.

Sync and security

If you link several devices, your library is encrypted on your device before it is sent to the relay. Of your sync, the relay only keeps data it cannot read, and the key never goes through it in the clear. If the relay asks for it before creating a sync, a Cloudflare anti-bot check (Turnstile) loads at that point: it reads your IP address and information about your browser.

If you find a security flaw, write to contact@mirinu.ch before making it public.

Public profile

The “Share my profile” setting, off at first, publishes a read-only page on the Mirinu relay: the name you pick and, for each title of the chosen types, its name, year, poster, status, whether it is in your favorites and your rating out of 10, with totals and the day of the last update. The app updates it at most once an hour, when your library changes. It is the only data Mirinu keeps in the clear: anyone with the link can read it. Your personal notes, watch dates and devices are never in it. The link is drawn at random, the page is closed to search engines, and its posters come from TMDB's servers.

This sharing rests on your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR). Stopping it withdraws that consent: the app erases the page from the relay as soon as it reaches it, and keeps trying while offline; someone who opened it may keep it for up to a minute in their browser. A page the app has not updated for 180 days is erased. The relay then keeps a fingerprint of the link, and nothing else, for 2 years so it cannot be used again.

Posters you suggest

You can suggest a poster for a movie, a show or a manga, with or without an account. The app shrinks the image on your device and sends it to the relay, which redraws it as two WebP images without metadata. Nobody sees it before it has been checked; once accepted, it shows for everyone. The relay keeps the image, the title it is for, the day it was sent and the state of the poster. An image waiting with no decision is erased after 60 days.

For a report, the relay keeps the day and a salted fingerprint of the block of network addresses it came from, for 30 days at most. For a refused image, it keeps a fingerprint of its pixels for 90 days. For an image removed for good, it keeps two fingerprints of the image and the title with no time limit, so that it does not come back. The relay's backups, which last 7 days, contain the posters.

The relay never keeps your IP address, your country, the bot check token, your device or your account. It works out your country from your IP address when you send, without keeping it, to refuse sends from Russia, using IP Geolocation by DB-IP (https://db-ip.com, license CC BY 4.0). The bot check is Turnstile, by Cloudflare: it reads your IP address and information about your browser.

Comments from Trakt

Mirinu shows public comments from Trakt, read-only; it writes none. To show them, the app asks the relay for the comments of a title, and the relay asks Trakt with the title's number, with nothing that identifies you. An image or a GIF in a comment only loads after you tap it, from the site that hosts it, which then sees your IP address. Each comment's menu lets you report it and hide its author.

For a report, the relay reads the comment again at Trakt and keeps its number, its text and the addresses of its images, its author's public username, the title where you saw it, the reason you chose, the day and a salted fingerprint of the block of network addresses the report came from. It erases the text at Marco's decision, or after 60 days with no decision, and the fingerprint and the reason after 30 days at most. When three different networks have reported a comment, it is hidden for everyone until that decision. If Marco hides a comment or an author, the relay keeps with no time limit the comment's number or the author's username, so that it does not come back, until Marco undoes it. If he leaves a comment visible, the relay keeps its number for 90 days. The relay's backups, which last 7 days, contain this data.

A comment you report and an author you hide are also remembered on your device, to hide them. This list is neither synced nor backed up, and hiding an author sends nothing to the relay. The relay never keeps your IP address, your country, the bot check token, your device or your account. The country check and the bot check are the ones described for posters.

With an account

The account is only for people aged 15 or over. It lets you get your library back if you lose your device, and manage your devices from any browser. Like sync, it serves the service you ask for.

The relay keeps your email address, encrypted, and an unreadable fingerprint that is used to recognize you; if you sign in with Google, an unreadable identifier of your Google account and its address, encrypted, but neither your name nor your photo. It also keeps the list of your devices (type and dates, to the day), your encrypted library and key envelopes, which it cannot open. Your recovery code never leaves your devices: the relay keeps only values computed from it, which cannot be used to recover it. To get everything the relay keeps about your account in a file, choose “What the server keeps about me” in Account and sync.

Sign-in codes and security notices, such as a new device or an account deletion, are sent from contact@mirinu.ch, through Infomaniak (Switzerland). A code email says what type of device and which country the request came from: the relay deduces that country from your IP address with the IP Geolocation by DB-IP database (CC BY 4.0 licence), without keeping it. Mirinu never writes to advertise. If you sign in with Google, Google knows that you use Mirinu; on app.mirinu.ch, Google's button loads as soon as the sign-in screen opens, and Google then sees your IP address. Google adheres to the Data Privacy Framework.

Before a code is emailed, a Cloudflare anti-bot check (Turnstile) reads your IP address and information about your browser. The relay also asks Cloudflare whether the domain of your address (what follows the @) receives email; neither your full address nor your IP address goes there. To curb abuse, the relay also counts requests per email address and per IP address, in an unreadable form erased the next day. These protections rest on the legitimate interest in protecting the service.

A sign-in code is valid for 10 minutes. In a browser, a sign-in lasts 30 days, or 24 hours on a shared computer. Account data, with the encrypted copy of your library attached to it, stays as long as the account exists. You can delete it whenever you want: it is erased 7 days after your request, or right away from a linked device, then leaves the relay's backups within 7 more days. An account with no sign-in or sync for 24 months is erased, after a warning email sent 30 days before, when Mirinu has an address to reach you.

Providers and countries

The relay runs on a server rented from Infomaniak (Switzerland), which also hosts the contact@mirinu.ch mailbox. The same server serves mirinu.ch and app.mirinu.ch, without keeping a log of visits. Cloudflare (United States) is used only for the Turnstile bot check and to check the domain of an email address. For signing in with Google, Google (United States) is involved too. Cloudflare and Google take part in the Data Privacy Framework, which Switzerland and the EU recognize for data sent to the United States.

Legal bases and retention

Mirinu follows the Swiss data protection act and, for people in the EU, the GDPR. Sync and showing title pages serve the service you ask for (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR). Matching and hosting rest on the legitimate interest in running the service (Art. 6(1)(f)). The anti-bot check and the per-address count rest on the same legitimate interest: protecting the service against abuse.

Mirinu does not keep your IP address: to curb abuse, the relay counts requests per address, in an unreadable form erased the next day. It erases a sync without an account 180 days after its last sync, and a space left empty after one day. The relay's nightly backups keep what is erased for up to 7 more days. An email you send is kept while your request is handled.

Your rights

You can find out what Mirinu keeps about you, have it corrected or erased, receive it in a file, object to a processing or have it restricted. Write to contact@mirinu.ch: the answer comes within 30 days. Your library can be exported from the app, and the “Delete your data” page explains how to erase everything.

You can also contact the Federal Data Protection and Information Commissioner (FDPIC) or, in the EU, the authority of your country.
